Rubric 101

A rubric is your firm's definition of a complete brief for one kind of work: what has to be known before anyone puts a number on it. Leadership writes it once. Every inquiry gets held to it, whoever catches it.

Three Steps to Firm Ground

1

Set the bar for how you sell.

Tick the engagement models your firm actually uses and set the floor for each. The floors test the weakest criterion, never the average, so a strong brief with one hole doesn't clear the bar.

2

Define what a complete brief must contain.

Start from the built-in rubric and edit it into yours. Each criterion is a plain description of what you need to know, plus ideal answers in your own words. That's what every inquiry gets measured against.

3

Make it available to everyone.

One rubric per line of business. Every inquiry is read against all of them, so nobody picks a rubric before uploading, and nobody applies the standard differently.

1

Upload whatever the client sent.

A 38-page RFP, a budget spreadsheet, a photo of a whiteboard. It's read as one inquiry, not file by file, and FirmGround works out which kind of work it is. You don't classify anything first.

2

Get the score and the verdict.

What's missing, criterion by criterion, where each answer came from, and what the material supports selling right now. One opportunity, as many question rounds as it takes; the score climbs as answers arrive.

3

Close the gaps, question by question.

Each question is tied to a gap, compiled into an email you can edit. Copy it for the client, answer it yourself, or let FirmGround assume. Assumptions are marked, and never priced as fact.
